# D5 — screen-grade Δ-probe contamination: floyd's "high" Δ was a CF-1-violation timing artifact

**Step:** 1.3 (GAP-2, survivor non-flatness). **Date:** 2026-06-30. **Status:** fixed, validated.
**Commit:** `7d21485` (carries both the contaminated screen raw and the corrected endpoint raw).
**Raw:** `results/characterization/delta_probe/floyd_warshall.json` (screen, contaminated) vs
`floyd_warshall__endpoint.json` (clean). Recompute: `delta_aggregate_final.py`.

## Summary

The Step-1.3 screen Δ-probe reported `_shortest_path`/floyd Δ_all = **3.188** — a HIGH (non-flat) unit,
the second-largest in the corpus. On the v1.4 median-of-3-subprocess **endpoint** rig the same 16 configs
gave Δ_all = **1.338** (FLAT). The screen value was a **measurement-contamination artifact**: two of floyd's
16 configs (SPLT, SPLA) timed at 786/810 ms against a true ~296 ms — a 2.6× inflation on exactly the two
points that set the worst/best ratio. Because the screen rig (single in-process median, built for scale
SELECTION, not RQ1 endpoints) has no cross-subprocess robustness layer, a transient co-runner load spike
survived into those configs' medians. Corrected by re-measuring on the endpoint rig under `measure_wrap` +
CF-1 (verified-quiesced host). The contamination would have flipped the §1.3 corpus verdict.

## Symptom

```
screen  floyd_warshall.json : SPLT 785,825,338 ns  SPLA 809,850,436 ns  (all other 14 cfgs 254–387 ms)
                              => Δ_all = 809.85/254.0 = 3.188   (best SPHT, worst SPLA)   "HIGH"
endpoint floyd_warshall__endpoint.json : all 16 cfgs 250–336 ms
                              => Δ_all = 335/250 = 1.338        (best SPHT, worst SCLA)   "FLAT"
```
Only the two `SP*L` configs (checks-on · cdivision=False · −O1) were anomalous on screen; every other config
matched the endpoint within noise. An internal physical contradiction was visible in the screen raw itself:
UPHT (boundscheck OFF) = 308 ms was *slower* than SPHT (boundscheck ON) = 254 ms — impossible for a real
directive effect, a tell that the slow points were noise, not signal.

## Root cause

Two coupled mechanisms, one trigger (concurrent containers during the early-session run):

1. **CF-1 violation at measurement time.** floyd's screen probe ran at ~03:18 during the session's
   duplicate-container / power-generation incident (multiple `podman` measurement+`mb_powergen` containers
   alive at once — the same incident that was clobbering the power-table output). CF-1 (ONE container on the
   isolated core at a time) was momentarily violated, so a co-runner stole the shared DRAM channel during the
   window in which SPLT/SPLA were timed.
2. **The screen rig has no robustness layer.** `gate_runner scale-probe` is a SINGLE in-process median over
   the reps — explicitly labeled `"IN-PROCESS scale-selection timing, NOT an RQ1 endpoint"`. With one
   subprocess and no median-of-N, a load spike that lands within a config's reps corrupts that config's
   median outright; there is no second/third subprocess to out-vote it. floyd at N=700 is
   **memory-bandwidth-bound** (the N×N distance matrix streams N times), so it is maximally sensitive to a
   co-runner stealing bandwidth — which is why floyd, and not the compute-bound units, took the hit.

## Why it produced the symptom (and why it was dangerous)

floyd was *projected* HIGH a priori (a dense O(N³) triple-loop, expected to vectorize). The contaminated
3.188 **confirmed the projection** — the worst kind of error, because confirmation bias makes a plausible-
looking artifact easy to accept. With floyd at 3.188 the corpus Δ_all median was **1.513 ≥ 1.5 → a MARGINAL
§1.3 PASS**. With floyd corrected to 1.338 the median is **1.338 < 1.5 → FLAT (FAIL)**. The single
contaminated unit would have flipped the terminal Phase-1 verdict from a correct FLAT to a false admission —
the most consequential error available at this decision point.

## Fix

- Re-measure every borderline/high unit on the **v1.4 median-of-3-subprocess endpoint rig** under
  `measure_wrap` (verifies no_turbo=1, governor=performance, cpu3 isolated, THP∈{madvise,never}) + **CF-1**
  (one container at a time, enforced by serial runners `run_delta_probe_endpoint.sh`). Two independent
  defenses: the median-of-3-subprocess layer out-votes a single anomalous subprocess; CF-1 prevents the
  co-runner in the first place. (`scripts/corpus/delta_probe_endpoint.py`, calls `measure_endpoint` directly.)
- The screen probe is retained as raw but is NEVER the admission number: the final aggregate
  (`delta_aggregate_final.py`) prefers `__endpoint.json` per unit and labels the rig provenance.
- Addresses the root cause (contended wall-clock + no robustness layer), not the symptom: the fix is a rig
  discipline, not a per-number patch.

## How it was found

The human's GAP-2 directive — "re-measure the borderline/high Δ on the endpoint rig; screen-grade can
mis-rank the ratio across the 1.5 threshold." floyd was selected precisely because it was the high unit most
able to move the median and had only been screen-measured. The endpoint rig immediately reproduced ~296 ms
for SPLT (vs the screen's 786 ms), exposing the artifact. Hypotheses rejected en route: a real −O1→−O3
vectorization lever (disproved — checks-off configs were not faster, and the effect vanished on the clean
rig); a genuine cdivision penalty on SP*L (disproved — cdivision is irrelevant to floyd's division-free
min-plus loop).

## Why it slipped through

The screen Δ-probe was run mid-session for *coverage* (get all 9 units a fast first number) during the exact
window the duplicate-container incident was live; CF-1 was not re-verified per-config the way the endpoint
rig enforces it. The screen rig was designed and labeled for scale-selection, where a 2.6× outlier on 2/16
points is immaterial; reusing it as the admission Δ — where one config sets the worst/best ratio — was the
latent risk. **Control evidence that this is contamination, not a systematic screen/endpoint bias:** csr
(screen 3.279 → endpoint 3.183) and binning (screen 1.699 → endpoint 1.696) agree to ~3% — the two rigs
match wherever the screen was taken cleanly; only floyd's two contended configs diverge.

## Validation

- floyd endpoint Δ_all = 1.338 / Δ_strict = 1.179; all 16 configs 250–336 ms, no internal contradiction
  (`floyd_warshall__endpoint.json`).
- CONTROL units re-measured on the same rig reproduce their screen values (csr 3.18, binning 1.70, predictor
  1.41, lda 1.30) — the rig agrees where the screen was clean.
- Corpus verdict recomputed from the all-endpoint aggregate: median Δ_all 1.338, Δ_strict 1.253 → FLAT on
  both axes (`delta_aggregate_final.py`), robust and no longer dependent on a screen-grade number.

## Why this is a contribution, not just a fix

This is a **timing-rig methodology result**: it demonstrates, with a controlled before/after, that
single-shot in-process medians on a memory-bound kernel can be contaminated by a transient CF-1 violation by
2.6× — enough to invert a pre-registered admission decision — and that a median-of-N-subprocess endpoint rig
plus enforced single-container isolation removes it. It is the fourth silent defect in the project's
instrument-validation record (after the predecessor's D1 zero-embedding and D2 bare-import, and this
project's in-place re-callability defect, v1.4 §5.1), and the only one that would have flipped a scientific
verdict had the rig not caught it.
